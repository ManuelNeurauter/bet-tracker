# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary user: a sports bettor tracking their own activity on a personal instance. May eventually share the tool with other bettors. The user is analytically minded — they care whether they're actually profitable, not just whether they won last week. They use the app after placing bets and periodically to review performance. Context is focused and deliberate: this is a discipline tool, not entertainment.

## Product Purpose

BetLedger gives bettors a clear, honest record of their betting activity. It serves three jobs equally: accountability (am I actually profitable over time?), analysis (which sports, bookmakers, and bet types yield the best ROI?), and record-keeping (a reliable log of every bet placed). Success means the user can answer any question about their betting history in seconds and trust that the numbers are right.

## Brand Personality

Professional, clean, trusted. Like a fintech app — Robinhood or Trading 212 energy. Polished, credible, and serious without being cold. The tool feels like something a disciplined bettor would pay for, not a spreadsheet dressed up with CSS.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboards**: pastel cards, flat icons, bland Bootstrap grids that look like every admin panel. No low-contrast placeholder text, no rounded cards with nothing to say.
- **Spreadsheet / utility tools**: Google Sheets energy — functional but joyless, no visual craft, no sense that the tool respects the user's time.
- **Bookmaker sites**: loud gambling-casino energy, garish colors, promotional noise. BetLedger is the opposite: it's where you go to find out if the bookmakers are taking your money.

## Design Principles

1. **Numbers first.** Data density is a feature. Every layout decision should make metrics easier to read and compare, not harder.
2. **Earn the premium feel.** Polished means precise — consistent spacing, deliberate hierarchy, no accidental visual noise. Premium is in the craft, not in decoration.
3. **Discipline over entertainment.** This is a tool for honest self-assessment. The interface should feel calm and clear, never exciting or promotional.
4. **Trust through accuracy.** Contrast, legibility, and correct number formatting are non-negotiable. If a user has to squint at a figure, the design failed.
5. **Shareable from day one.** Even as a personal tool, every screen should look like it was built for someone else to use. No rough edges, no "good enough for just me" compromises.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the floor. Body text must hit ≥4.5:1 against its background. Interactive elements need visible focus states. Charts should not rely solely on color to convey meaning (labels or patterns as supplement). Support reduced motion where animations are used.
